01 · Keep your books

What do I actually need to record?

Make the story behind each amount easy to find.

Read the guide

Keep a clear record of business income and expenses, with supporting documents. For an expense, record the date, who you paid, the amount, what you bought, and its business purpose. Keep the receipt or invoice and proof of payment together.

For example: A $110 equipment rental entry names the supplier, rental date, equipment, job, and payment method. A card statement can support payment; the rental record explains what it was for.

Monthly totals can become a quarterly summary. If the three months show $2,000, $3,000, and $5,000 of income, the quarter is $10,000. Count the underlying income once; the summary is another view of it.

Your next step: Pick one transaction and collect its supporting records.

02 · Understand your taxes

Can I pay a little as I earn?

A payment habit can fit your working life.

Read the guide

Federal income tax is paid through the year through withholding or estimated payments. If you need to make estimated payments, the IRS allows weekly, biweekly, or monthly payments, provided enough is paid by the required payment deadlines.

For example: Moving $100 into your own tax savings account builds a reserve. Paying $100 to the IRS creates a tax payment. Keep those amounts separate in your records.

Your estimate depends on income, deductions, credits, withholding, and other taxes such as self-employment tax. A flat savings percentage is a planning starting point, not a calculation of what you owe. The four estimated-tax payment periods are not all three months long.

Your next step: Review the current Form 1040-ES worksheet and payment dates. Save confirmations for payments you make.

03 · Prepare throughout the year

Why wait until tax season to start?

Small reviews now give you fewer loose ends later.

Read the guide

Set aside a few minutes each month to review your income, expenses, and supporting documents. Compare your records with bank and cash activity. Mark anything that needs an explanation instead of guessing.

For example: A bank deposit might include customer income and money you moved from another account. Identify each part before treating the full deposit as business revenue.

HA’s goal is to carry your Bookin’ records into a HATax draft as the year progresses. You would still review personal details, other income, eligibility, missing information, and the finished return before authorizing filing. That complete connection is being built.

Your next step: Make a short list of this month’s unanswered questions and keep it with your records.

04 · Get help

What if a receipt is missing?

Start with what happened and what you can support.

Read the guide

Write down the transaction while you remember it: date, amount, payee, item or service, business purpose, and how you paid. Label it as a missing-receipt item. Ask the supplier for a duplicate and gather related invoices, messages, or payment records.

For example: For a cash rental, keep your dated note and seek the supplier’s rental record or confirmation. Be clear about which details come from documents and which come from memory.

A handwritten note can be part of your records. Whether the evidence supports a tax deduction depends on the expense and applicable rules; some expenses have specific documentation requirements. A preparer may need to ask questions or help reconstruct the records.

Your next step: Gather the available evidence and bring the unresolved item to your preparer for review.

A few good questions.

Can I file a return here today?

No. HA is in development. The tax workspace is a calculation and research preview, and bookkeeping access is locked until protected sign-in is configured. These previews do not prepare a complete return or submit one to the IRS.

Does HA move money automatically?

No. A reserve scenario is an illustration, not a bank transfer or a tax payment. Banking connections and authorized payment workflows are part of the longer-term plan.

How will my books connect to my taxes?

The goal is to use the income and expense records you enter through the year to build your HATax draft. You will review the information, answer outstanding questions, and authorize filing separately. The complete connection is still being built.
