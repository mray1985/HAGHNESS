# HA / HATax: optional refund-to-retirement pilot

October 2, 2026. Internal discussion draft, not sent. No partnership, endorsement, API access or funding arrangement is confirmed.

## Proposed discussion

HA is developing HATax to help clients enter tax documents, understand their return and make informed decisions about their refund. We would like to explore an optional pathway for eligible clients to direct an amount they choose into a Schwab traditional or Roth IRA.

The client benefit is a clear opportunity to save while reviewing a refund, with account ownership, eligibility, contribution year and paperwork explained before proceeding. Participation would be voluntary. Clients would retain the option to receive their refund elsewhere. Investment selection would remain within Schwab's authorized process.

HA would contribute the tax-workflow experience, plain-language explanations, eligibility review and a record of the client's choices and consent. Schwab could contribute its approved account-opening process, custodian requirements, funding instructions and support. A possible benefit to Schwab is reaching clients who want to begin or continue retirement saving; HA has no verified acquisition-volume forecast.

We request an exploratory discussion with the appropriate Schwab team to determine whether a limited pilot is feasible, what program or agreement applies, and what technical and operational support would be available.

## Proposed pilot design

Start with fictional accounts and documents. After security, recovery, tax eligibility and provider approvals are verified, consider a small voluntary cohort; 20 clients is a planning suggestion, not a commitment or enrollment forecast.

1. Explain traditional/Roth differences and review eligibility, compensation, income limits, other contributions and the intended year using the applicable year's rules.
2. Let the client choose the amount, including zero, and confirm which account they own.
3. Complete Schwab's required account application, identity verification, account agreement and funding instructions through the approved provider process.
4. Record separate client consent for any data disclosure or authorized funding action. Agree the exact consent documents with Schwab before pilot use.
5. Verify that the custodian accepts the intended refund deposit and contribution year. Use the applicable return instructions for whole-refund or split-refund routing.
6. Track pending, received, rejected and changed deposits separately. Confirm the actual contribution amount and year with the custodian before treating funding as complete.
7. Follow up on delays, refund offsets, changed amounts and any needed tax-return or contribution correction.

The initial design can use a provider-approved referral and client-controlled account process. Automated account opening or money movement would require a verified agreement and technical contract. HA would not hold client funds under this proposal.

## Questions for Schwab

- Which team and program can evaluate this proposal? Are tax-software referrals eligible, and what restrictions apply to compensation, marketing and use of Schwab's name?
- Which accounts accept federal refund deposits, and what exact routing and account instructions apply?
- How does the client designate a prior-year contribution, obtain confirmation and resolve a late or rejected deposit?
- Which application, custodian agreement, disclosure and authorization documents are required? Who retains them, for how long, and how can consent be withdrawn?
- Is a sandbox or approved integration available for account opening, funding status and contribution-year confirmation? What support and escalation routes apply?
- What charges apply to the client and HA, including exceptions, implementation and support? What service commitments can be quoted?
- What minimum data is needed, through which secure channel, and which party is responsible for access controls, recovery and incident handling?

## Success measures and current readiness

Measure voluntary participation, completed applications, verified funded accounts, actual contributed amounts, contribution-year accuracy, client understanding, abandonment and support incidents. Record observed task and settlement times separately. Do not promise investment results, refund arrival dates or a ten-minute complete filing experience.

HATax currently has a local form-entry preview and connected-ledger/document work. Hosted MFA, production document storage, filing, comprehensive tax calculations and provider funding are not verified. A live pilot must wait for those applicable controls and agreements.

The [IRS refund-to-IRA FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/irs-procedures/refund-inquiries/refund-inquiries-12) explains that refunds may go to traditional or Roth IRAs, excludes SIMPLE IRAs, and requires timely deposit for prior-year treatment. The IRS does not label the contribution year; the owner must notify the custodian and verify limits. Delays and offsets can require corrections. [Form 8888](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8888) supports splitting a refund; whole-refund instructions differ.

[Schwab's IRA information](https://www.schwab.com/ira) describes its accounts. Its public information does not establish an HA partnership or automated funding permission. The [public contact page](https://www.schwab.com/contact-us) is a possible routing starting point, not a verified partnership contact.
